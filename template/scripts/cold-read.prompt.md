You are writing a **cold read** of the product in this repository: an independent view of what it is, who it is for
and what is riskiest, written before its makers' own strategy work can shape yours. Someone will compare your read with
theirs later, so your independence is the whole value. Read the repository and write one Markdown document; *Delivery*, at
the end, says where it goes.

## What you must not read

Skip these, and anything whose purpose is positioning, brand, messaging, landing copy or a strategy method:

- `Roadmap/00-strategy/` (all of it): the makers' own strategy and any earlier cold read
- the `strategy` skill folder (and, on an older install, any `pmf-narrative`, `north-star`, `risk-validation` or
  `cold-read` skill folder), and the `refine` skill's `references/coaching.md` and `references/strategy.md`: the
  coaching methods
- `references/`, `brand/`, landing-page and marketing copy, positioning or messaging modules
- `.env*`, secrets, `node_modules/`, `.git/`

If you open something and then realise it is on this list, stop reading it and say so under *Contamination*.

## What you may read

The README, `AGENTS.md`, the code, the product's roadmap and its shipped work, issues, and the public web (competitors,
analogs, current market facts). If you cannot reach the web, say so once and work from the repository.

## The document

Write exactly these sections, in this order, as `##` headings:

1. **Header**: today's date, and the line "Cold read, written without the strategy files or the coaching methods".
2. **Reading log**: a table of what you read (files, depth); a table of what you skipped deliberately and why; and a
   **Contamination** paragraph naming anything you saw that the list above excludes, even a summary line. This section
   is mandatory: a read without it cannot be trusted.
3. **What this product is**: one sentence, then the evidence from the code.
4. **Who it is for**: the first customer, and later ones.
5. **The problem, in the customer's words**: their need, not the product's features. Every claim cites a source or is
   labelled *hypothesis*.
6. **Market and competitors**: what they would use instead today, including doing nothing.
7. **Positioning**: the category, and one line on how it differs.
8. **Growth and business model**: the first channels, and how it could charge.
9. **Riskiest assumption and the cheapest test**: in your own words, the one belief that sinks the rest if it is wrong,
   and the cheapest test that would tell. This section is mandatory.
10. **Confidence and open questions**.
11. **Sources**: every link you used.

Plain, short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. Never invent a figure; "couldn't find" is an answer.
